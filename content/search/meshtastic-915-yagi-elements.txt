Supplemental Instructions 
for 
915 MHz Meshtastic Yagi Antenna Build 
 
While the basic instruction set for this build is complete as far as it goes, it does not really 
go far enough.  This is due to the fact that the instruction set was developed and written 
as applicable to the assembly of the antenna from the kits that were provided to the 
GCARC Club members involved in the original build event.  Those kits included pre-cut 
and finished element rods for the antenna.  Thus, that instruction set did not reference 
cutting and finishing the element rods in any manner whatsoever, and in fact, the 
instructions never actually identified the specific lengths of the elements.  While that data 
can be gleaned from a careful read of the YagiCad ® screen capture provided, which 
shows the element lengths and positions, it was decided to develop and provide these 
specific element preparation instructions. 
Based on the lengths of the individual element rods, a total of three of the twelve-inch 
brass rod stock recommended for this build will be needed if constructing a single 
antenna.  If constructing multiple antennas (or preparing kits for such a build), it is possible 
to use less than three full rods for a single antenna, resulting in less waste rod stock.  We 
will discuss this concept more in depth later on.

The preparation of the element rods requires the use of a strong cutting pliers, preferably 
end-cutting pliers.  In addition, a means must be available to square off the end of the 
element rod where the rod was cut.  This is best done with a Dremel ® tool with a cut-off 
wheel in its tool collet.  While it can also be done with a good file, that is a very tedious 
and dirty job.  An ultrafine-point Sharpie® marker will be needed for marking the rod stock 
during measurement, and an accurate means of measuring to the nearest thirty-second of an inch is needed.  I recommend a steel rule such as the Mr. Pen Steel Rulers offered 
by Amazon ( https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B07ZS4JSQP) for a very reasonable 
price.  These rulers have the advantage of clear and accurate markings with no offset of 
the “zero” position.  This means that you can simply line up one end of the rod stock with 
the end of the ruler and go from there. 
Once all of the required equipment is at hand, start by marking out the element lengths 
on the rod stock as follows: 
 on the first rod, measure and mark 6-9/32 ” from one end of the rod, and then 
measure and mark 5-17/32 ” from the opposite end of the rod, and set that rod 
aside; 
 on the second rod, measure and mark 5-21/32 ” from one end of the rod, and then 
measure and mark 5-19/32 ” from the opposite end of the rod, and set that rod 
aside; and 
 on the third rod, measure and mark 3-1/32 ” from one end of the rod, and then 
measure and mark 3-1/32” from the opposite end of the rod, and set that rod aside. 
The next step is to rough-cut the element rods, based upon the marks that were just 
made.  If the above instructions were followed properly, each rough-cut rod will end up 
having one square finished end after the rough cuts are made. 
Using the end-cutting pliers, cut each piece of rod stock about a thirty-second of an inch 
away from the measured mark towards the center of the rod.  This means that you will 
be making the first cut between the two marks on the rod stock, and then the second cut 
will be made between the remaining measured mark and the cut end of the rod stock.  
Once all three pieces of rod stock have been cut near each of the measured marks, the 
yield will be six element rods which will each have one finished end, one rough end, and 
each will be about a thirty-second of an inch longer than its measured and marked length. 
Finish each element rod by grinding or filing off the rough-cut end, bringing the cut to a 
clean square finish and the element rod to its specified finished length.  Lay the rod 
against the ruler periodically as you grind or file it to ensure compliance with the length 
requirement for the element rod on which you are working at that time. 
Finally, dress of any flash that may be “mushrooming” the cut end of the rod to give a nice 
clean and round element rod end that will fit properly into the hole on the boom.  This will 
complete the preparation of the element rods from three twelve-inch lengths of brass rod 
stock, for a single antenna construction project. 
If you are in the position of needing to prepare element rods for multiple antennas to be 
built, for example, if you are preparing kits for future use in building the antenna in 
quantity, some reduction in rod stock waste can be achieved.  In this case, you would be 
laying out and cutting the element rods for three antennas using only eight lengths of rod 
stock instead of nine lengths as would be used in the single-antenna process. Begin by measuring and marking three sets of reflector and director (or parasitic) element 
rods in the same manner as was described in the single-antenna procedure.  That means 
that you will be measuring and marking out the element lengths on the rod stock as 
follows: 
 on the first rod, measure and mark 6-9/32 ” from one end of the rod, and then 
measure and mark 5-17/32 ” from the opposite end of the rod, and set that rod 
aside; and 
 on the second rod, measure and mark 5-21/32 ” from one end of the rod, and then 
measure and mark 5-19/32 ” from the opposite end of the rod, and set that rod 
aside. 
Repeat that measure and mark sequence to get three sets of rods, which yields four 
elements each for three antennas.  Rough-cut and then finish these element rods as 
described previously. 
Next, measure and mark two lengths of rod stock at four-inch intervals, effectively marking 
the rod stock in thirds.  Cut these rod segments at each of the marks, which will give you 
six four-inch rods segments. Four of those rod segments will be finished at one end, while 
the remaining two rod segments – the ones cut from the centers of the rod stock lengths 
– will be rough-cut on both ends.  Using the Dremel® tool or the file, dress and finish one 
end of each of the two rods that have both ends cut. 
Continue by measuring and marking, working from the finished end of the rod 
segments, 3-1/32” from the finished end of each of the six four-inch rod segments.  Then, 
rough-cut those rod segments at about 1/32” past the mark towards the cut end of the rod 
segment.  Finally, using the tool of your choice, grind or file the rod segments to their 
finished element rod length of 3-1/32” as was done with the parasitic elements earlier. 
This completes the element rod preparation procedure when multiple antennas are being 
prepped, or when kits are being assembled.  Note that a standard drinking straw makes 
a great storage tube for the finished element rods.  Fold over and staple one end of the 
drinking straw, slide the element rods into the straw, and then fold over and staple the 
second end of the straw closed.  This keeps the element rods together and prevents them 
from poking their way out of a plastic bag that may be used to contain all of the parts that 
comprise an antenna kit.
